# Caterly Operational Module Blueprint

## Purpose

This document extends `CATERLY_PRODUCT_BLUEPRINT.md` and refines the optional-module architecture after reviewing modern restaurant operations products and considering how the same capabilities apply to catering, restaurants, commissaries, venues, and multi-location hospitality groups.

The central decision is important: **capabilities that create value across both catering and restaurant operations should not be buried inside the Restaurant module.** They should be reusable operational modules connected to the same Caterly data graph.

Caterly remains an **Operational Intelligence System** governed by:

- **FAST** - Caterly never gets in the operator's way.
- **CONNECTED** - Enter information once and let it flow everywhere it is needed.
- **INDUSTRY-NATIVE** - Caterly behaves the way an experienced hospitality operator thinks.

Core is still built and locked first. Nothing in this document changes the Phase 1 Core priority.

---

# 1. Revised Module Architecture

The long-term module family should distinguish between business-model-specific capabilities and shared operational capabilities.

## Business-model modules

- Caterly Restaurant
- Caterly Venue
- Caterly Leads Pro
- Caterly Client Portal

## Shared operational modules

- Caterly Food Cost
- Caterly Inventory
- Caterly Supply / Purchasing
- Caterly Invoice Intelligence
- Caterly Production
- Caterly Workforce
- Caterly SOPs & Training
- Caterly Billing & Payments Automation
- Caterly Rentals
- Caterly Logistics
- Caterly Analytics
- Caterly Automation
- Caterly Accounting Integrations

A restaurant, caterer, commissary, venue, or hybrid operator can enable the shared modules that match its operation.

This avoids making a catering company purchase a Restaurant module merely because it wants stocktakes, waste tracking, invoice ingestion, or inventory control.

# 2. Caterly Food Cost

Purpose: transform recipes and ingredient pricing into live cost and margin intelligence.

Features:

- Ingredient master
- Recipe and sub-recipe costing
- Recipe yields
- Portion yields
- Trim/cook/yield percentages
- Recipe-unit and purchase-unit conversions
- Current ingredient cost
- Historical ingredient cost
- Recipe cost
- Portion cost
- Menu food-cost percentage
- Target food-cost percentage
- Contribution margin
- Suggested retail/selling price
- Cost-change alerts
- Margin-change alerts
- Event theoretical food cost
- Restaurant theoretical food cost
- Menu engineering
- Cost by event/menu/category/location

## Actual vs theoretical food cost

Food Cost becomes significantly more valuable when connected to Inventory and POS/event demand.

Theoretical usage comes from what Caterly believes should have been consumed based on recipes and demand.

Actual usage comes from inventory movement:

Beginning inventory + purchases + transfers in - transfers out - ending inventory = actual usage.

Caterly can compare:

- Theoretical COGS
- Actual COGS
- Dollar variance
- Percentage variance
- Ingredient-level variance
- Location variance
- Event/category/menu variance where attribution is possible

This should become a major operational KPI rather than a buried report.

Purpose: provide fast, location-aware inventory control for catering kitchens, restaurants, commissaries, warehouses, bars, and venues.

Inventory is separated from Restaurant because catering operations also need it.

## Inventory features

- Inventory item master linked to ingredients/products
- Inventory locations
- Storage areas within locations
- On-hand quantity
- Inventory valuation
- Count units
- Purchase units
- Recipe units
- Unit conversions
- Lot/expiry information where useful
- Par levels
- Reorder points
- Inventory adjustments
- Inventory movement history
- Location transfers
- Receiving integration
- Production consumption/output integration
- Waste integration
- Stocktake history

## Stocktakes

Stocktaking should be designed as an extremely fast operational workflow.

Features:

- Scheduled stocktakes
- Full or partial counts
- Count sheets generated by location/storage area
- Mobile/tablet-friendly counting
- Numeric-keypad-first entry
- Keyboard-first desktop entry
- Count progress
- Multiple counters where appropriate
- Variance from expected on-hand
- Approval/finalization
- Inventory valuation at count time
- Historical count comparison

The operator should be able to walk a cooler, freezer, dry store, bar, warehouse, or catering kitchen and enter counts with minimal taps.

## Waste tracking

Waste is a first-class inventory movement, not merely a note.

Capture:

- Item/ingredient/prepared product
- Quantity
- Unit
- Location
- Date/time
- Employee/user
- Cost
- Reason
- Optional event/order/production reference
- Optional note/photo later

Suggested reasons:

- Spoilage
- Expired
- Overproduction
- Prep waste
- Quality rejection
- Customer return
- Dropped/damaged
- Incorrect preparation
- Event overage
- Other

Waste data should flow into actual COGS and operational intelligence.

## Inter-location transfers

Support movement such as:

Warehouse -> Catering Kitchen
Central Kitchen -> Restaurant A
Restaurant A -> Restaurant B
Catering Kitchen -> Event staging

Transfers should reduce one location and increase another with one connected transaction rather than two independent adjustments.

## Demand-driven purchasing

Recommended purchasing should eventually combine:

- Confirmed catering event demand
- Restaurant forecast demand
- Production requirements
- Current inventory
- Pars/safety stock
- Open purchase orders
- Internal transfers

Conceptually:

Forecast demand + confirmed event demand + required safety stock - usable inventory - inbound supply = recommended purchase requirement.

This is one of Caterly's strongest potential differentiators because catering bookings provide unusually strong forward demand signals.

Caterly should close the purchasing loop:

- Invoice without PO

# 5. Caterly Invoice Intelligence

Purpose: turn supplier invoices into structured operational data without depending exclusively on supplier APIs.

This should be a small shared module because it benefits restaurants, caterers, commissaries, and venues.

## Invoice ingestion

Potential inputs:

- Email forwarding/import
- PDF upload
- Image/photo upload
- Supplier API/electronic invoice where available

Extract/reconcile:

- Supplier
- Invoice number
- Invoice date
- Product/SKU
- Description
- Pack size
- Quantity
- Unit price
- Extended price
- Taxes/fees
- Credits
- Invoice total

## Matching

Map invoice lines to Caterly supplier products and ingredients.

Confidence should be visible. Low-confidence mappings require human review rather than silently changing costs.

## Price intelligence

An accepted invoice can update:

Supplier price -> ingredient cost -> recipe cost -> menu food cost -> event/restaurant margin -> analytics.

Flag meaningful price movements and preserve history.

## Reconciliation

Where Supply is enabled, compare invoices against purchase orders and receiving records.

Where Accounting Integration is enabled, approved invoice data can flow downstream without re-entry.

## Resilience rule

Automated invoice processing is asynchronous. A failed document parse must never block normal Caterly operation.

# 6. Caterly Production

Purpose: convert combined demand into executable kitchen work.

Production is shared between catering, restaurants, commissaries, venues, bakeries, and central kitchens.

Features:

- Multi-event production planning
- Restaurant forecast production demand
- Combined demand planning
- Batch recipes
- Sub-recipes
- Batch scaling
- Prep quantities
- Prep deadlines
- Required-by times
- Station assignments
- Prep assignments
- Production status
- Completion tracking
- Yield recording
- Waste recording
- Production labels
- Consolidated daily/weekly prep
- Event/location allocation
- Prepared-product inventory

## Central production kitchen / commissary

Treat a commissary as a producing location capable of fulfilling demand from other locations and events.

Example:

Restaurant A requires 40 L demi-glace.
Restaurant B requires 25 L.
Saturday catering requires 60 L.
Sunday catering requires 35 L.

Caterly consolidates demand to 160 L, determines batch requirements, calculates ingredients, considers inventory, generates purchasing requirements, schedules production, then allocates finished product back to destinations.

## Internal production orders

Locations should be able to request product from a central kitchen. Caterly can consolidate requests into production batches and track fulfillment/transfer.

## POS integration

Restaurant should integrate with POS platforms rather than attempting to become a POS.

Potential platforms should be prioritized by customer demand and API availability.

POS data can provide:

- Sales

This is a core differentiator.

Restaurant forecast demand and known catering-event demand feed the same production, inventory, purchasing, labour, and analytics graph.

Caterly should know both what history suggests will happen and what confirmed future events guarantee will happen.

# 9. Caterly Workforce

Purpose: connect labour demand to people and schedules across event-based and weekly operations.

Features remain as defined in the primary blueprint, with stronger connections to Restaurant and Production.

Catering model:

Event -> service/production requirements -> shifts -> staff.

Restaurant model:

Sales forecast -> labour requirement -> weekly schedule -> staff.

Combined operator model:

Restaurant demand + catering events + production demand -> total labour requirement -> schedule.

This allows a hybrid hospitality company to understand total labour demand rather than scheduling restaurant and catering teams in disconnected systems.

# 10. Caterly Analytics / Operational Intelligence

Purpose: turn connected operational data into decisions and recommended actions.

Expand the existing Analytics blueprint to include:

- Actual vs theoretical COGS
- Inventory variance
- Waste percentage and cost
- Waste by reason/location/item
- Supplier price movement
- PO/receiving/invoice variance
- Stocktake variance
- Transfer activity
- Production yield variance
- Forecast vs actual sales
- Forecast vs actual labour
- Food cost by location
- Prime cost by location
- Cross-location benchmarking
- Commissary performance
- Purchasing savings/opportunities

## Intelligence progression

Caterly should progress through four levels:

1. **Record** - what happened?
2. **Explain** - why did it happen?
3. **Predict** - what is likely/known to happen next?
4. **Recommend** - what should the operator do about it?

Example:

Instead of only reporting that short-rib cost increased, Caterly can eventually say:

- supplier cost increased 6.2%;
- menu food cost moved from 27.9% to 29.6%;
- 1,140 portions are already booked over the next six weeks;
- current selling price misses the company's 28% target;
- suggested new selling price is $50.75 for future bookings.

Recommendations should show the underlying facts so operators can trust and override them.

# 13. Revised Commercial Packaging Principle

Do not force capabilities into Restaurant merely because restaurants commonly use them.

Examples:

A catering-only company may want:

- Food Cost
- Inventory
- Supply
- Invoice Intelligence
- Production
- Workforce

without Restaurant.

A restaurant-only company may want:

- Restaurant
- Inventory
- Food Cost
- Invoice Intelligence
- Supply
- Workforce

without event-heavy catering workflows beyond Core requirements.

A hybrid group may enable nearly all of them.

This creates smaller, clearer value packages and allows Caterly to scale from a focused catering operator to a complex hospitality group without maintaining separate product stacks.

Potential mini-module pricing units:

- Inventory: per location
- Invoice Intelligence: company/location plus document volume if needed
- SOPs & Training: company/location or active employee tier
- Food Cost: company/location
- Supply: company/location
- Production: producing location/commissary
- Restaurant: restaurant location
- Workforce: company plus active employee component
- Analytics: company/location tier

Commercial packaging can later bundle related modules while keeping technical boundaries independent.

# 14. Revised Development Principle

These additions do **not** move ahead of Caterly Core.

The development order remains:

1. Lock Core.
2. Prove Core under real catering operations.
3. Build the shared ingredient/recipe/cost foundation.
4. Add modules in the order that produces the most connected operational value.

A likely post-Core dependency sequence is:

Food Cost
-> Inventory
-> Supply / Invoice Intelligence
-> Production
-> Workforce
-> Analytics
-> Restaurant

The exact commercial release order can change based on customer demand, but data architecture should respect these dependencies.

# 15. Expanded Product North Star

Caterly should not merely tell an operator what happened.

**Caterly should understand what is going to happen and help the operator determine what needs to happen next.**

A restaurant-only system can infer future demand from history.

A catering system knows substantial future demand from booked events.

Caterly can combine both.

Example:

Next seven-day beef requirement: 184 kg

- Restaurant forecast demand: 72 kg
- Confirmed catering demand: 126 kg
- Current usable inventory: 31 kg
- Incoming purchase orders: 40 kg
- Recommended additional requirement: 127 kg before safety-stock adjustments

The same demand model can inform purchasing, production, labour, inventory, logistics, food cost, and profitability.

That is the long-term meaning of Caterly as an **Operational Intelligence System**.
